History & life · Rome, Italy

A city that never lives in just one era

← Product

You turn a corner and there it is — a line of columns two thousand years old, and a scooter buzzing past so close you feel the wind of it, and coffee steam drifting from a doorway. This is how Rome works. The old empire isn't tucked behind a fence, kept apart from the day; it lives right in the middle of everything. A temple wall holds up somebody's front room. Laundry sways above a cracked foundation. Children chase a ball across marble that emperors once crossed. In other cities, history sits behind glass; here it simply keeps living, between the washing and the shopping. You notice you're never in only one moment at a time. Today's clock ticks over a clock of centuries, and nobody finds it strange. Perhaps that's the secret of this place: it never decided which age it belongs to, and it never had to.
