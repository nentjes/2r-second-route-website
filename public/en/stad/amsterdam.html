Water & trade · Amsterdam, Netherlands

The city that made its streets from water

← Product

You're leaning on the rail of a low bridge, and the street beneath you is made of water. A bicycle bell, the soft knock of a boat against its mooring, and rows of narrow houses lean gently toward the canal as if to hear what passes. Look up: those tall, thin facades wear stepped gables and little hooks beneath the roof, and every one of them stands on a forest of wooden piles driven deep into soft, wet ground. To build here, you first had to strike a bargain with the mud. That is really how this city rose — not on stone, but on agreement and trust. Merchants, craftsmen and newcomers from every shore set their money, their skills and their beliefs side by side and turned the mixture into trade. They dug these canals as streets, not to shut the world out but to let it in. Tolerance, you realise, was simply good business.
